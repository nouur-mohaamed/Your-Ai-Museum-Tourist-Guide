Ramesses II, known as Ramesses the Great, ruled Egypt from 1279 BC to 1213 BC, a 66-year reign. Born to a military officer, he became pharaoh at 25. He expanded Egypt’s territory through numerous campaigns in Syria, Nubia, and Libya. Ramesses II is noted for his long reign, military prowess, and construction of significant monuments like Pi-Ramesses. He celebrated 13 or 14 Sed festivals. He died at around 90 years old and was initially buried in KV7, later moved to the Royal Cache. His mummy is now displayed at the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Cairo.
Ramesses II, during his reign from 1278 to 1259 BC, engaged in several military campaigns. He decisively defeated Sherden pirates and incorporated them into his royal guard. His first Syrian campaign in 1276 BC captured Amurru. In 1274 BC, he fought the Hittites at Kadesh, ending in a stalemate. He later campaigned in Syria, capturing various cities including Damascus. In 1259 BC, he concluded a peace treaty with the Hittites at Kadesh, addressing disputes over Canaan and exiling Mursili III. The treaty, in both Egyptian hieroglyphs and Hittite cuneiform, aimed to maintain peace and honor their deities.
During Ramesses II's reign, Egypt maintained control over Phoenician coastal towns like Sumur under Egyptian rule. Ramesses II led campaigns into Nubia and along the Mediterranean coast, establishing fortresses and maintaining control. His Nubian campaigns included battles against southern tribes and showcasing his sons' involvement. Key events include his first Sed festival in Year 30, celebrating 30 years of reign. Ramesses II secured Egypt’s borders, maintained peace, and oversaw significant construction projects.
Ramesses II celebrated 13-14 Sed festivals, more than any other pharaoh. He initiated extensive building projects from the Delta to Nubia, focusing on monuments like Pi-Ramesses, the Ramesseum, and Abu Simbel. The Ramesseum features a 17m high statue and hypostyle hall. Abu Simbel elevates him to divine status. Ramesses also expanded gold mining operations. Recent discoveries include a 3,200-year-old Memphis colossus and a royal platform in Matariya.
In December 2019, a rare red granite bust of Ramesses II was unearthed in Giza. In May 2023, a fragment of Ramesses II’s granite sarcophagus was identified. A golden sword inscribed with his name was found in September 2024. Ramesses II died around age 90 after a 67-year reign in early Year 67 of his rule, suffering from multiple health issues. He was originally buried in KV7, moved to other tombs due to looting, and rediscovered in 1881. His red hair, symbolically linked to the deity Set, was confirmed natural through microscopic examination. His mummy was transferred to the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in 2021. In 2006, tufts of his hair were stolen and later recovered.
Ramesses II, who reigned from approximately 1279 to 1213 BC, lived about 90 years. He originated from the northeastern nome of Egypt and was the son of Seti I and grandson of Ramesses I. His queen was Nefertari, and he had sons like Amun-her-khepeshef and Khaemwaset. Physically, he stood 1.7 meters tall with red, wavy hair that turned white. He suffered from dental issues, arthritis, and heart disease, leading to his death in Year 67 of his reign likely due to a jaw abscess. Initially buried in KV7, his mummy was relocated multiple times before being rediscovered in 1881. It has since been displayed in the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Cairo.
