Tutankhamun, born Tutankhaten, ruled from 1332 to 1323 BC, succeeding Smenkhkare and Neferneferuaten. He was the son of the male mummy from KV55 (likely Akhenaten or Smenkhkare) and "The Younger Lady" (a full sister to KV55, daughter of Amenhotep III and Queen Tiye). His wet nurse was Maia. Tutankhamun married Ankhesenpaaten, who later became Ankhesenamun. He fathered two stillborn daughters. 

He acceded at 8 or 9, changing his name to Nebkheperure and moving the capital back to Memphis, restoring Thebes and traditional worship. He changed his and his wife’s names, ending Atenism and reinstating Amun’s cult. His reign marked the end of the Eighteenth Dynasty’s royal line.
Tutankhamun, who ruled Egypt from approximately 1332 to 1323 BC, initiated the Sphinx Avenue and completed Amenhotep III’s Luxor Temple decorations. He also began constructing a royal tomb using recycled blocks from Akhenaten’s temples. Diplomatically, he reestablished relations with Mitanni. His military equipment suggests active warfare. Tutankhamun died at about 18 due to malaria and a leg fracture. He was buried in a small non-royal tomb in the Valley of the Kings. Discovered in 1922 by Howard Carter, his tomb contained over 5,000 artifacts. He was deified during his lifetime, with temples built in Nubia dedicated to Amun. His treasures were exhibited globally and are now preserved at the Grand Egyptian Museum.
Tutankhamun was worshiped as a local incarnation of Amun in Nubia, serving both religious and administrative purposes. His cult mirrored Amun's traditional hierarchy, with Viceroy Huy as its head. Notable figures included Khay (First Prophet) and Mermose (Second Prophet). The cult had a dedicated harem at Kawa, separate from Amun's harem. In Thebes, a stela from Karnak Temple confirmed his cult's presence there, with inscriptions equating his ka with Amun's divine status. Tutankhamun suffered from severe malaria and a compound leg fracture, which likely contributed to his death.
The analysis of Tutankhamun’s remains through microscopic wear and genetic testing concluded that his artifacts were royal regalia, not mobility aids. Genetic testing also ruled out several syndromes previously suggested. Mild scoliosis was likely due to post-mortem handling. Physical evidence indicates he was lean, standing about 167 cm tall, with excellent dental health. Reconstructed faces varied; 2005 models faced criticism for skin tone choices, while 2014 depictions were criticized as sensationalized. Genotyping revealed paternal haplogroup R1b and maternal haplogroup K, linking him to migrations into Africa and Near Eastern origins. Autosomal STR analysis showed significant genetic affinities with Sub-Saharan African populations, highlighting the Nile Valley's historical role as a crossroads.
Tutankhamun's Death:
- No direct ancient records exist.
- Main theory: Multiple health issues, including a femur fracture and severe malaria.
- Disproved theories:
  - Murder hypothesis: Bone fragments found were from modern unwrapping, not a fatal blow.
  - Chariot accident: Missing chest wall was stolen by tomb robbers.

Historically, Tutankhamun was born around 1342 BC, changed his name to reflect restored traditional Egyptian religion, and belonged to the Thutmosid royal line of the Eighteenth Dynasty. His father was likely Akhenaten or Smenkhkare, and his mother was a full sister of Akhenaten. He was married to his half-sister Ankhesenpaaten, who later became Ankhesenamun.

He ruled for 9 years, shifting the capital back to Memphis/Thebes, ending Atenism, and restoring foreign relations. He was also a skilled warrior with extensive weaponry and military victories. He was one of the few pharaohs deified during his lifetime, with dedicated temples in Nubia and Thebes.
